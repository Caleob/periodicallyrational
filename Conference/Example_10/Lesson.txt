# Lesson 10: Geometric Algebra & Kinesthetic Substitution

### 1. Make Abstract Variables Visible Shapes
* Secondary students often treat algebra as meaningless letter manipulation ($ax^2 + bx = c$).
* Prompt the AI to ground symbols in physical geometry: an $x^2$ term is an actual square, an $x$ term is a linear strip, and adding or subtracting represents sewing on a flap or cutting out a corner.
* Visualizing algebra spatially builds intuitive number sense before introducing formal formulas.

### 2. Animate the Evaluation (Fly Numbers Into Place)
* Don't just display a static "Incorrect" or "Too Low" label when a student submits a guess.
* Ask the AI for kinesthetic number flow: animate the guessed value replacing each $x$ in the equation, calculate the resulting area step by step, and fly the evaluated result into an interactive tracking table.
* Showing *how* an equation evaluates demystifies function notation and numerical approximation.

### 3. Include Impossible Cases (No Solutions)
* Most textbook problem generators only present neat, integer-factorable quadratics.
* Prompt the AI for boundary levels—like configurations with negative discriminants where no real dimensions can produce the target area.
* Teaching students to recognize when and why a problem has no real solution fosters genuine mathematical reasoning.
