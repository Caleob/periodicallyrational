# Example 10: The Rug Problem (Geometric Quadratic Equations)

## What is this app?
This is an interactive mathematical manipulative that bridges concrete geometry and abstract quadratic equations. It represents quadratic equations of the form $ax^2 \pm bx = c$ as physical Persian rugs with woven texture patterns. Students determine the unknown width $x$ of a rug given its dimensions ($a_1 x$ by $a_2 x$), a cut-out or added strip, and its net area.

## How to use it in a virtual K-12 classroom
* **Visualizing the Unknown:** Rather than memorizing the quadratic formula mechanically, students see $x^2$ and $x$ as tangible geometric regions (an $x$ by $x$ square, or an $a_1 x \times a_2 x$ rectangle with an added flap or cutout).
* **Animated Substitution:** When students enter a guess for $x$, the app visually animates the substitution—highlighting the $x$ terms in the formula, computing the evaluated left-hand side, and gliding the number into the guess-tracking table with directional feedback arrows (too high / too low).
* **Scaffolded Levels for Differentiation:**
  - **Level 0 ($x^2$):** Pure square root exploration ($x^2 = c$).
  - **Level 1 (Concrete):** Real-world rugs with physical dimensions and positive real solutions.
  - **Level 2 (Abstract):** Advanced quadratics with decimal solutions.
  - **Level 3 (No Solutions):** Visual exploration of quadratics with negative discriminants ($D < 0$) and why no real rug exists.
  - **Level 4 (Negative Area):** Conceptual boundary cases where the target area is negative.
* **Screen Sharing & Guess-and-Check:** Perfect for live Zoom/Teams demonstrations where students propose guesses in the chat, narrowing the interval until they reach the solution within $0.01$.
